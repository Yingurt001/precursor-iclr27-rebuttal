# General Response

We thank the three reviewers for their careful reading. Reviewer $\color{red}{\text{Tery}}$ found the paper readable and well structured, Reviewer $\color{blue}{\text{wxGK}}$ valued the separation of three questions often conflated in early-warning evaluation and the controlled isolation of credit transfer across onset, and Reviewer $\color{green}{\text{pSK8}}$ found the problem interesting and understudied, with research questions that are nicely posed. Below we summarize the points raised by more than one reviewer and the new analyses behind our answers. Each reviewer's thread gives the full tables, and the revised PDF reports all of them.

*Line, table and section numbers refer to the submitted PDF.*

# 1. Validity of the precursor detectability test
Reviewers wxGK and pSK8 asked whether the test detects precursors rather than drift or annotation offsets, and whether it depends on its descriptors and settings. Four new analyses address this.

**1. A descriptor-free test.** A kernel test based on the maximum mean discrepancy, with the same placebo calibration, detects 46 of 47 annotated 3W transitions against 43 for the descriptor test, and 36 of 500 benchmark events against 70. A test that is more sensitive where precursors exist and finds fewer benchmark events shows that the sparsity of benchmark evidence does not come from the descriptors.

**2. One setting at a time.** Under the corrected calibration split, varying each setting of Tab. 1 gives 60 to 90 P events, and SMD, PSM and SWaT exceed their placebo rate in every setting while MSL and SMAP never do.

**3. Drift in the 3W control.** Normal operation of the 3W wells changes over hours, so windows far from the normal reference can be flagged without any fault. Against normal operation shortly before the transition, the test detects the first ten minutes of 11 of 19 transitions and flags 4.1% of normal stretches under the same rule. Normal windows as far from their reference as the windows tested in the submission, the last 100 seconds before each fault, are flagged in about half of the cases, so the revision restates the 3W control at the start of the transitions.

**4. Annotation offsets on PSM and SWaT.** The onset-continuity check of Tab. 8 classifies 6 of the 7 PSM P events and 7 of the 9 SWaT P events as boundary shifts, in which the largest jump precedes the annotated onset. Benchmark-level evidence of pre-onset deviations therefore rests mainly on SMD, where 38 of 51 P events show a jump at the annotated onset.

# 2. Interpretation of the negative model results
Reviewers wxGK and pSK8 asked what non-significance excludes and whether MPA is the right measure.

**1. Training and aggregation.** With the SMD P events, matched controls and selection control fixed, neither per-machine training (+0.049 [−0.049, 0.155]) nor the near-onset peak (+0.022 [−0.007, 0.054]) alone changes iForest MPA, and the per-machine peak of 0.810 does not exceed its selection control of 0.758. The revision replaces the per-machine comparison of §4.3 with this design.

**2. Excluded effect sizes.** On SMD, mean-score MPA of 0.66 or higher is excluded for every detector, while values below about 0.65 remain open. The revision states these bounds next to every significance result and limits the abstract to the interpretable cells.

**3. Threshold-free comparison.** A point-wise AUC truncated at onset ranks the detectors as MPA does (Spearman 0.96 on SMD), whereas the untruncated AUC rewards post-onset detection, for example 0.836 against 0.507 for iForest on SWaT.

# 3. Presentation
Reviewers Tery and pSK8 pointed out terms used before their definition and decisions stated without their reason. The revision defines onset, pre-onset interval, placebo calibration, the event classes, exchangeability, the selection control and the rejection rate at their first use, gives the regularized covariance below Eq. 1, replaces causal by online, and ends each results section with the answer to its research question. It also explains the 100-step window, the longest that keeps 500 of the 537 events evaluable, and states every sparsity result for the 100 steps before onset.
